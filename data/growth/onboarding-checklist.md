# Growth Onboarding Checklist

Last updated by: amaka@nimbuslabs.io — Priya reviewed but hasn't signed
off on the CRM section yet, treat that part as provisional (see the note
inline).

Pair this with `general/new-hire-first-week.md`.

## Week 1

- [ ] Get CRM (Hubspot) access — should be automatic on day 1
- [ ] Get Figma viewer access, request editor access if your role needs it
      (see `general/tooling-and-access-requests.md`)
- [ ] Read `brand-guidelines.md` before writing anything customer-facing
- [ ] Read `crm-rules.md` — especially the record ownership and data
      handling sections, these get enforced

## Week 2

- [ ] Shadow a campaign brief being written, using the format in
      `campaign-process.md`
- [ ] Get added to the ad platform seats you'll need (paid ad account
      ownership is requested separately, not automatic)
- [ ] Meet with Finance briefly if your role touches paid spend — ask
      Priya who the right Finance contact is, this isn't standardized
      yet

## First campaign

Your first campaign brief should be reviewed by your manager before
submission to the planning board, even though the formal process in
`campaign-process.md` doesn't require manager pre-review for everyone.
This is a new-joiner-specific step, not a permanent one — after your
first two or three campaigns, submit directly per the normal process.

## CRM section (provisional, not yet fully agreed)

Draft note from Amaka: new joiners should probably shadow a full record
lifecycle (create, update, transfer, delete-request) before touching
production CRM data solo, but this isn't official policy yet — Priya
wants to fold it into a broader CRM training doc first. Follow it as
guidance for now, not as a hard requirement.

## Budget approvals — read this before your first spend request

Campaign budgets that clear 5,000 USD need Finance approval, not just
Growth lead approval (`campaign-process.md`). If you're running paid
media specifically, also read `paid-media-policy.md` — it has a lower
threshold for a specific reason explained there.
